A Birthday Party

Last month, my little brother Nam turned eleven. For weeks before his birthday, he talked about nothing else. Every evening at dinner, he asked us, "How many days until my birthday?" And every evening, my mother answered with a smile, "Not long now." Nam wanted a big party with all his friends from school. My parents agreed, but only if he helped to plan it. So the whole family started to prepare.

The first thing we did was make a list of guests. Nam wanted to invite his whole class, which has thirty-five students. My mother laughed. "Our house is not big enough for thirty-five children!" she said. "You can invite ten friends." Nam thought about it for a long time. It was very difficult for him to choose. In the end, he chose eight friends from his class and two boys who live on our street. He also wanted to invite our cousins, our aunt and uncle, and of course, our grandmother, who lives with us.

Next, Nam made the invitations himself. He did not want to buy cards from a shop. He wanted to draw them. He spent a whole Saturday afternoon at the kitchen table with colored pencils, paper, and stickers. On each card, he drew a big cake with eleven candles, some balloons, and a picture of a football, because football is his favorite sport. Inside, he wrote: "Please come to my birthday party! Saturday, at three o'clock, at my house. There will be cake, games, and lots of fun!" He wrote each friend's name very carefully on the front of the card. Some of the letters were not very straight, but the cards were beautiful. The next Monday, he took them to school and gave them to his friends.

My parents and I planned the food. Children love sweet food, but my mother also wanted to give them something healthy. So we decided to make a mix of both. For the main food, we would have small sandwiches, fried chicken, spring rolls, and fruit. For drinks, we would have orange juice, water, and milk tea. And of course, there would be a birthday cake. Nam wanted a chocolate cake with football decorations on top. My aunt is very good at baking, so she offered to make it as her birthday gift to him.

On the morning of the party, the whole house was busy. My father and I cleaned the living room and moved the sofa and the table against the walls to make space for games. My mother and my grandmother cooked in the kitchen. The house smelled wonderful: fried chicken, warm bread, and fresh fruit. Nam was so excited that he could not stay still. He ran from room to room, asking, "Can I help? Can I help?" Finally, my mother gave him a job. She asked him to blow up thirty balloons. That kept him busy for a long time. By the time he finished, his face was red, and he was out of breath, but he was very proud.

We hung the balloons from the ceiling and on the walls. We also put up a big sign that said "Happy Birthday, Nam!" in bright colors. My father put a colorful paper cloth on the table and arranged the plates, cups, and napkins. My grandmother made a small bunch of flowers from her garden and put it in the middle of the table. When everything was ready, the room looked like a different place. It was bright, colorful, and full of happy energy.

At a quarter to three, my aunt arrived with the cake. It was even better than we had imagined. It was a big, round chocolate cake with green cream on top that looked like a football field. In the middle, there were two small plastic football players and a tiny ball. On the side, she had written "Happy Eleventh Birthday, Nam" in white cream. When Nam saw it, his mouth fell open. "Wow!" he shouted. "It's the best cake in the world!" He hugged our aunt so hard that she almost dropped her bag.

The guests began to arrive at three o'clock. Nam stood by the front door and welcomed each friend with a big smile. Every friend brought a present wrapped in colorful paper. Nam put all the presents on a small table in the corner. He wanted to open them right away, but my mother told him to wait until after the cake. "Patience is a good thing to learn," she said. Nam did not look very happy about that, but he agreed.

When all the children had arrived, my father organized some games. The first game was musical chairs. We put nine chairs in a circle, one less than the number of children. My father played music on his phone, and the children walked around the chairs. When the music stopped, everybody had to find a chair and sit down quickly. The child without a chair was out. Then we took away one more chair and started again. The children laughed and screamed and pushed each other. In the end, a small, quiet girl called Linh won. Everyone was surprised, because she was the smallest child in the group, but she was also the fastest.

The second game was a treasure hunt. Before the party, my father had hidden ten small bags of candy in different places in the house and on the balcony. He gave the children some clues on small pieces of paper. For example, one clue said, "I am cold inside, and I keep your food fresh." The children thought for a moment, and then one boy shouted, "The fridge!" They all ran to the kitchen, and there was a bag of candy on top of the fridge. Another clue said, "You sit on me to watch television." That was the sofa. The children loved this game. They ran all over the house, shouting and laughing, until they found every bag.

After the games, everyone was hungry. The children sat around the table and ate sandwiches, chicken, and spring rolls. They drank a lot of juice. My grandmother walked around the table and put more food on everyone's plate. "Eat, eat," she said. "Growing children need a lot of food." The parents who came with their children sat in the kitchen with my mother and drank tea. They talked about school, homework, and how fast their children were growing.

Then it was time for the cake. My mother turned off the lights and closed the curtains. My aunt lit the eleven candles on the cake and carried it slowly into the living room. Everybody started to sing "Happy Birthday." Nam sat at the end of the table, his face shining in the light of the candles. When the song finished, my mother said, "Make a wish, Nam!" Nam closed his eyes for a few seconds. Then he took a big breath and blew out all eleven candles at once. Everyone clapped and cheered. Later, I asked him what his wish was, but he would not tell me. "If I tell you, it won't come true," he said.

My aunt cut the cake into pieces, and everyone got a slice. It was delicious: soft, sweet, and full of chocolate. Some of the children got cream on their noses and faces, and everyone laughed. One boy ate three pieces and then said his stomach hurt. His mother shook her head and said, "I told you so."

Finally, it was time to open the presents. Nam sat on the floor, and his friends sat around him. He opened each present slowly and carefully. He got a new football, two books, a puzzle, a toy car, some colored pens, a shirt from his favorite football team, and a board game. Our cousins gave him a small toy robot that could walk and talk. My parents gave him a new bicycle, which they had hidden on the balcony. When Nam saw the bicycle, he jumped up and down and shouted with joy. He thanked everyone for every present, even the ones he did not like very much. My mother had taught him that it is important to be polite and grateful.

At about six o'clock, the parents came to take their children home. Each child got a small bag with some candy and a balloon to take home. Nam stood at the door and said goodbye to each friend. "Thank you for coming!" he said again and again. When the last guest had left, the house was suddenly very quiet. There were pieces of wrapping paper all over the floor, half-empty cups on the table, and balloons in every corner. It was a big mess, but nobody cared.

That night, Nam was very tired, but very happy. Before he went to bed, he came to my room and sat on my bed. "That was the best birthday ever," he said. "Thank you for helping." I hugged him and said, "Happy birthday, little brother." Then he went to his room and fell asleep in less than five minutes, holding his new toy robot in his arms. I sat in my room for a while and thought about my own birthdays when I was a child. I realized that the presents were never the most important part. The most important part was the people: my family, my friends, the laughter, the songs, and the feeling that I was loved.
