A Weekend at the Beach

Every summer, my family tries to take a short trip together. We are not rich, so we cannot travel to other countries or stay in expensive hotels. But we always find a way to go somewhere for two or three days. This year, we decided to go to a small beach town about four hours from our city. My father found a cheap guesthouse on the internet, only five minutes' walk from the sea. We booked two rooms for two nights. Everyone was very excited, especially my little brother, who had not seen the sea for three years.

We left early on Friday morning. My father had rented a car for the weekend, and he wanted to leave before the traffic got bad. My mother woke us up at half past four. It was still dark outside. We were all sleepy, but nobody complained. My mother had packed everything the night before: our clothes, swimsuits, towels, sun cream, hats, sunglasses, some medicine, and a big bag of food for the trip. My grandmother did not come with us, because long trips in the car make her feel sick. She stayed at home with my aunt, and she told us to bring her back some dried fish and a photo of the sunset.

The road out of the city was quiet so early in the morning. As the sun came up, we could see green rice fields on both sides of the road. There were farmers already working in the fields, wearing their traditional cone hats. We saw buffaloes walking slowly along the side of the road, and small villages with houses, temples, and markets. My brother had his face against the window the whole time. "Look! A buffalo! Look! A pagoda!" he shouted every few minutes.

At about seven o'clock, we stopped at a small restaurant by the road for breakfast. We ate noodle soup and drank strong coffee. The restaurant was simple, with plastic chairs and a metal roof, but the soup was delicious and very cheap. The owner, a friendly woman with a loud voice, asked us where we were going. When we told her, she smiled and said, "Oh, that is a beautiful place! You must try the seafood at the night market. And go to the lighthouse to watch the sunset."

We arrived at the beach town at about ten o'clock. As soon as we got out of the car, we could smell the sea. The air was warm and a little salty, and there was a light, cool wind. We could hear the sound of the waves. My brother wanted to run straight to the beach, but my mother said we had to check in at the guesthouse first.

The guesthouse was small and simple, but very clean. It was owned by an old couple, Mr. and Mrs. Tam, who had lived in the town all their lives. Mrs. Tam gave us cold coconut water as a welcome drink. Our rooms were on the second floor. Each room had two beds, a fan, a small bathroom, and a balcony. From the balcony, we could see the blue sea between the trees. "It's perfect," my mother said.

We changed into our swimsuits, put on a lot of sun cream, and walked to the beach. The sand was white and soft, and the water was clear and blue. There were not many people, because it was a small town and not very famous yet. There were some families with children, a few young couples, and some fishermen pulling their boats onto the sand. A long line of coconut trees grew along the beach, giving some shade from the hot sun.

My brother ran into the water with a big shout. My father followed him. My mother and I sat under a beach umbrella for a while and watched them. The waves were not too big, so it was safe for swimming. My father taught my brother how to float on his back. At first, he was afraid and kept putting his feet down. But after a few tries, he could float for almost a minute. He was so proud. "Look at me! I'm floating!" he shouted to us.

After a while, I joined them in the water. The sea was cool and refreshing after the long, hot drive. I swam out a little further, then turned and floated on my back, looking up at the sky. It was a deep blue, with a few white clouds. For a moment, I forgot about everything: my work, my exams, my worries. There was only the sound of the waves and the warm sun on my face. I felt completely free.

At lunchtime, we were all very hungry. We went to a small restaurant right on the beach. It was a simple place with a roof made of palm leaves and tables on the sand. We ordered grilled fish, steamed crabs, fried squid, a big plate of morning glory with garlic, and rice. Everything was very fresh. The fish had been caught that same morning. We ate with our hands and our chopsticks, and we laughed a lot. My brother had never eaten crab before. At first, he did not know how to open it, so my father showed him. Then he could not stop eating. "This is the best food I have ever eaten!" he said with his mouth full.

In the afternoon, it was too hot to stay on the beach, so we went back to the guesthouse and took a nap. At about four o'clock, when the sun was not so strong, we went for a walk around the town. It was a quiet, charming place. There were small houses painted in bright colors: yellow, blue, pink, and green. Fishermen were sitting in front of their houses, fixing their nets. Children were playing football in the street. Old women were selling fruit, dried fish, and seashells from small tables. We bought some dried fish for my grandmother, just as she had asked.

Then we followed the advice of the restaurant owner and drove to the lighthouse to watch the sunset. The lighthouse was on top of a small hill at the end of the beach. We had to climb about two hundred steps to get to the top. It was hard work, and we were all sweaty and out of breath when we arrived. But the view was worth it. From the top of the hill, we could see the whole town, the long white beach, the green hills behind, and the endless blue sea.

We sat on some rocks and waited. Slowly, the sun went down over the sea. The sky changed from blue to yellow, then orange, then pink, then a deep purple. The water shone like gold. Some fishing boats were going out to sea for the night, and their small lights began to appear on the water like stars. Nobody said a word. We just sat together and watched. I took many photos with my phone for my grandmother, but I knew that no photo could show how beautiful it really was. My mother held my father's hand. My brother leaned against my shoulder. It was one of the most peaceful moments of my life.

That evening, we went to the night market. It was much busier than the town during the day. There were dozens of food stalls selling all kinds of seafood: shrimp, crabs, clams, squid, and many kinds of fish. You could choose your seafood, and they would cook it for you in any way you liked: grilled, steamed, or fried with garlic and butter. We tried a little of everything. We also had sweet soup with beans and coconut milk for dessert. There was live music, and some people were dancing. My brother bought a small bracelet made of shells for our grandmother.

On Saturday, we spent the whole day at the beach. We swam, played in the sand, and built a big sandcastle. My father and my brother played football on the sand. My mother read a book under the umbrella. I took a long walk along the beach and collected beautiful shells. In the afternoon, we rented a small boat with a local fisherman and went out to a little island near the coast. The water there was so clear that we could see fish swimming around the rocks. The fisherman showed us how to catch fish with a simple line. My brother caught a small fish, but he felt sorry for it, so he let it go.

On Sunday morning, we woke up early to watch the sunrise from the beach. Then we had breakfast with Mr. and Mrs. Tam. They had made us sticky rice and fresh fruit. When we said goodbye, Mrs. Tam hugged my mother and said, "Please come back next year." My mother promised that we would.

The drive home felt much longer than the drive there. We were all tired and a little sunburnt, and nobody wanted to go back to the busy city. My brother fell asleep in the car with his bag of shells on his lap. When we got home, my grandmother was waiting at the door. We gave her the dried fish, the shell bracelet, and the photos of the sunset. She looked at the photos for a long time. "It looks just like the beach where I grew up," she said quietly. Then she smiled and said, "Next year, I will come with you. I will take a pill for my stomach, and I will see the sea again."
