Our Dog Lucky

For many years, my brother and I asked our parents for a dog. Every birthday, every holiday, and every time we saw a dog on the street, we asked again. And every time, our parents said no. "A dog is a big responsibility," my mother said. "You have to feed it, walk it, clean up after it, and take it to the vet. Who will do all that?" "We will!" we always answered. But our parents did not believe us. They thought we would lose interest after a few weeks, and then they would have to do all the work.

Then, one rainy evening three years ago, everything changed. My father was driving home from work when he saw a small puppy at the side of the road. It was wet, dirty, and very thin. It was shaking with cold and fear. There was no mother dog and no owner anywhere. My father stopped his motorbike and looked at the puppy for a long time. He knew that if he left it there, it would probably not survive the night. So he picked it up, wrapped it in his jacket, and brought it home.

When my father walked into the house with the puppy in his arms, my brother and I could not believe our eyes. We screamed with joy and ran to him. My mother was not so happy at first. "What is this?" she asked. "Where did it come from?" My father explained what had happened. My mother looked at the little puppy. It looked back at her with big, sad, brown eyes. And I saw her face change. "Well," she said slowly, "I suppose we cannot put it back on the street in the rain."

That night, we gave the puppy a warm bath. It was so dirty that the water turned brown. Under all the dirt, it had soft, light brown fur and a white spot on its chest. We dried it with a towel and gave it some rice and chicken. It ate everything very quickly, as if it had not eaten for days. Then it lay down on an old blanket next to my bed and fell asleep immediately. My brother and I sat on the floor and watched it sleep for a long time.

The next day, we had a family meeting to talk about the puppy. My parents agreed that we could keep it, but only if my brother and I promised to take care of it. We made a list of jobs. I would feed it twice a day and give it fresh water. My brother would walk it every morning and every evening. We would both clean up after it and help to give it a bath once a week. My parents would pay for the food and the vet. We all signed the list, like a real contract. Then we had to choose a name. My brother wanted to call it "Tiger," but my father said, "This little dog was very lucky that I found it. Let's call it Lucky." And so our dog became Lucky.

The first few weeks were not easy. Lucky was a puppy, and puppies are a lot of work. He did not know where to go to the toilet, so he made a mess on the floor many times every day. He chewed everything he could find: shoes, socks, books, the legs of the chairs, and even my father's glasses. At night, he cried because he was lonely, and nobody could sleep. One morning, my mother found that he had chewed a hole in her favorite bag. She was very angry. "This dog has to go!" she said. But then Lucky came to her, wagging his tail, and licked her hand. She sighed and said, "Okay. One more chance."

We took Lucky to the vet for a checkup. The vet was a kind young woman who loved animals. She examined Lucky carefully. She said that he was about two months old and that he was healthy, but a little too thin. She gave him some medicine for worms and his first vaccine. She also gave us a lot of advice about how to take care of a puppy: what food to give him, how much he should eat, how often to walk him, and how to train him. "The most important thing," she said, "is to be patient and kind. Never hit a dog. Dogs learn best when you reward them for good behavior."

So we started to train Lucky. Every time he went to the toilet outside, we gave him a small piece of food and said, "Good boy!" Every time he chewed something he should not chew, we said "No" in a firm voice and gave him one of his toys instead. It took a long time, but slowly, he learned. After about two months, he did not make any more messes in the house. He also learned to sit, to lie down, to stay, and to come when we called his name. My brother taught him to give his paw, which is his favorite trick. Whenever visitors come to our house, Lucky sits in front of them and holds up his paw. Everyone loves it.

Now Lucky is three years old. He is not a puppy anymore, but he still has a lot of energy. He is a medium-sized dog with soft brown fur, a white chest, and a long tail that never stops moving. He is not a special or expensive kind of dog. He is just a normal street dog. But to us, he is the most beautiful dog in the world.

Lucky is a very important part of our family now. Every morning, he wakes up before everyone else and waits by my brother's door. As soon as my brother opens the door, Lucky jumps on him and licks his face. Then they go for their morning walk around the neighborhood. In the evening, when my parents come home from work, Lucky runs to the door to meet them, wagging his tail so hard that his whole body moves. My father always says that no matter how bad his day was, Lucky's welcome makes him feel better immediately.

Lucky has also changed our family in ways that we did not expect. Before we had Lucky, my brother spent most of his free time playing games on his computer. Now, he spends much more time outside. He walks Lucky twice a day, plays with him in the park, and has made friends with other dog owners in the neighborhood. He has become more responsible too. He never forgets to walk Lucky, even when it is raining or when he is tired.

My mother, who did not want a dog at all, has become Lucky's best friend. She talks to him while she cooks, and he sits by her feet in the kitchen, waiting for small pieces of meat to fall on the floor. When she is sad or stressed, he puts his head on her lap. "He understands me better than anyone in this house," she says, and we all laugh, but I think she means it.

My grandmother was a little afraid of dogs at first, because she was bitten by a dog when she was a child. But Lucky was always gentle with her. He seemed to understand that she was old and needed to be treated carefully. He never jumped on her, and he always walked slowly next to her. Now, they are very close. In the afternoon, when everyone is at work or school, Lucky stays with my grandmother. He lies next to her chair while she watches television or takes a nap. She says that he keeps her company and makes her feel safe.

Of course, having a dog is not always easy. Dogs cost money. We have to buy food, toys, and medicine, and we have to take Lucky to the vet for checkups and vaccines. Last year, he ate something bad on the street and became very sick. He could not eat or drink for two days, and we were very worried. The vet bill was expensive, but my parents did not complain. "He is part of the family," my father said. "When someone in the family is sick, we take care of them."

Having a dog also means that it is harder to travel. When we go on holiday, we have to find someone to take care of Lucky. Usually, our neighbor's son looks after him. He loves dogs, and Lucky loves him too. But we always miss Lucky when we are away, and we always hurry home.

When I think about the day my father found Lucky, I sometimes wonder what would have happened if he had not stopped. Lucky would probably have died that night in the rain. And our family would have missed so much joy, so much love, and so many funny moments. My father named him Lucky because he was lucky that we found him. But now I think the truth is the opposite. We were the lucky ones. We were lucky that we found him.

There are many dogs and cats on the streets of our city, without homes or families. Many of them are sick, hungry, and scared. If you are thinking about getting a pet, I hope you will think about adopting one of them instead of buying an expensive one from a shop. They may not be famous kinds of dogs or cats, but they will give you just as much love. Maybe even more. Our Lucky is the proof.
