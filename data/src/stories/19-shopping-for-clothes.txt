Shopping for Clothes

I have never really enjoyed shopping for clothes. My sister Lan loves it. She can spend a whole day walking from shop to shop, trying on dresses, shoes, and jackets, and she never gets tired. For me, one hour in a clothes shop feels like a whole day. I never know what to choose, the changing rooms are always too small, and I usually leave without buying anything. So for the last few years, I have worn the same jeans, the same few shirts, and the same old pair of shoes. My sister often says that I dress like an old man.

But last month, I had a problem. My best friend was getting married, and he asked me to give a speech at the wedding. I needed to look nice. When I looked in my closet, I realized that I did not have anything suitable. My only suit was ten years old. I had bought it for my university graduation, and it was much too small for me now. My good shirts had small stains or missing buttons. My only pair of nice shoes had a hole in the bottom. I needed new clothes, and I needed them in two weeks.

I had no idea where to start, so I asked my sister for help. She was delighted. "Finally!" she said. "I have been waiting for this day for years." We made a plan to go shopping together on Saturday. Before we went, she asked me some questions. How much money did I want to spend? What colors did I like? Did I want a full suit, or just a jacket and pants? What kind of wedding was it? I told her that I had about five million for everything, that I liked dark colors, and that the wedding was in the evening, in a nice restaurant. She wrote everything down in her phone like a real professional.

On Saturday morning, we went to a big shopping mall in the city center. It had five floors and more than two hundred shops. There were shops for every kind of clothing: men's clothes, women's clothes, children's clothes, sports clothes, shoes, bags, jewelry, and much more. It was Saturday, so the mall was very crowded. There were families with children, groups of teenagers, and couples holding hands. Music was playing loudly in every shop. I already felt tired, and we had not even started.

My sister took my arm and led me to the men's section on the third floor. "First, the suit," she said. "That is the most important thing." We went into the first shop. It was very elegant, with dark wooden floors, soft lights, and suits hanging neatly on the walls. A young salesman in a perfect black suit came to help us. "Good morning," he said. "Can I help you?" My sister explained what we were looking for. The salesman looked at me from head to toe and said, "I think I have something perfect for you." He brought me a dark gray suit. It was beautiful. Then I looked at the price. It was eight million. That was much more than my whole budget. I thanked him politely, and we left the shop quickly.

The next shop was cheaper, but the suits did not look very good. The material was thin and shiny, and they looked cheap. My sister shook her head. "No," she said. "You will look like a waiter." We went to a third shop, and a fourth, and a fifth. In each shop, I tried on suits, and in each shop, there was a problem. One was too big. One was too tight. One had sleeves that were too long. One was a strange color, somewhere between green and brown. I was starting to lose hope.

Then, in the sixth shop, we found it. It was a dark blue suit, simple and classic. When I put it on and looked in the mirror, I was surprised. It fit me well, and I looked good. I looked like a different person. My sister clapped her hands. "That's the one!" she said. The pants were a little too long, but the shop assistant said that they could make them shorter for free. It would take two days. The price was three million, which was a little expensive, but it was on sale with twenty percent off. So in the end, it was two million four hundred thousand. I decided to buy it.

The shop assistant measured my legs very carefully and wrote the measurements on a small card. She told me I could come back on Monday afternoon to pick up the suit. I paid at the counter with my bank card, and she gave me a receipt. "Please keep the receipt," she said. "You will need it when you come back."

Next, we needed a shirt and a tie. My sister said that a white shirt was the best choice for an evening wedding, because it goes with everything. We found a shop that sold only shirts. There were hundreds of them, in every color and size. I tried on a few white shirts and chose one made of soft cotton that was comfortable and fit well. It cost four hundred thousand. Then we looked at ties. I wanted a simple black tie, but my sister said that black was too serious. "This is a wedding, not a funeral," she said. She chose a dark red tie with small white dots. At first, I was not sure, but when I tried it on with the shirt and the suit jacket, I had to agree that it looked great.

By now, it was lunchtime, and we were both hungry. We went to the food court on the top floor of the mall. There were many small restaurants selling food from different countries: Vietnamese, Japanese, Korean, Thai, Italian, and American. My sister chose Korean food, and I chose a bowl of beef noodle soup. While we ate, my sister checked her list. "We still need shoes, a belt, and socks," she said. I groaned. My feet were already tired. But my sister promised that shoes would be the last thing.

After lunch, we went to a shoe shop on the second floor. I wanted comfortable shoes, because I knew I would be standing for a long time at the wedding. The shop assistant showed me several pairs of black leather shoes. I tried them on and walked around the shop in each pair. Some were too tight. Some were too hard. Finally, I found a pair that was comfortable and looked nice. They were nine hundred thousand. I also bought a black leather belt and a pack of dark socks.

When we added everything up, I had spent almost four million. It was less than my budget, so I was happy. My sister was very proud of herself. "You see?" she said. "Shopping is not so bad when you have a good plan and a good helper." I had to admit that she was right. Without her, I would probably have bought the first suit I saw, or I would have gone home with nothing.

On Monday afternoon, I went back to the shop to pick up my suit. The pants were now the perfect length. When I got home, I put on everything together: the suit, the shirt, the tie, the belt, the socks, and the shoes. I looked in the mirror for a long time. I almost did not recognize myself. I looked like a confident, successful man. I took a photo and sent it to my sister. She answered with ten hearts and the words "So handsome!"

On the day of the wedding, many people told me that I looked very nice. My best friend laughed when he saw me. "Who are you, and what have you done with my friend?" he said. My speech went well, and many people came to talk to me afterwards. I felt good about myself, and I think my new clothes were part of the reason. When you look good, you feel more confident.

Since then, I have learned a few things about shopping for clothes. First, have a plan before you go: know what you need and how much you want to spend. Second, take someone with you who has good taste and will tell you the truth. Third, always try clothes on before you buy them, and walk around in shoes before you choose. Fourth, buy fewer things, but buy good quality. One good suit is better than three cheap ones. And fifth, take a break for lunch. Shopping is hard work.

I still do not love shopping as much as my sister does. But now, at least, I do not dress like an old man. And I have promised my sister that I will go shopping with her again next month. She wants to help me buy some new everyday clothes. I am a little scared, but I trust her.
