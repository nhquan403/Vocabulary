Cooking Dinner Together

Last Friday, my friends and I decided to cook dinner together at my apartment. There were four of us: me, my roommate Linh, our friend Tuan, and his girlfriend Anna. Anna is from Australia, and she has lived in Vietnam for two years. She teaches English at a language school, and she loves Vietnamese food. For a long time, she had asked us to teach her how to cook some of our favorite dishes. So we finally made a plan.

We met at the supermarket at five o'clock in the afternoon. Before we went, we wrote a menu and a shopping list. Our menu had four dishes: fresh spring rolls, fried fish with tomato sauce, morning glory with garlic, and a simple chicken soup. For dessert, we wanted to make a sweet mango drink. It was a lot of food for four people, but we were all very hungry, and we wanted to have leftovers for the weekend.

At the supermarket, we took a cart and divided the list. Tuan and Anna went to buy the fish, the chicken, and the shrimp. Linh and I went to the vegetable section. We needed lettuce, herbs, cucumbers, carrots, tomatoes, garlic, onions, morning glory, limes, and chili. We also bought rice paper for the spring rolls, rice noodles, a bottle of fish sauce, and a bag of sugar. Anna also wanted some cold drinks, so we bought a few bottles of juice and some soda.

When we got to the checkout, the total was a little high. "This is more expensive than eating at a restaurant," Tuan said with a laugh. But Linh said, "Yes, but cooking together is more fun. And we will have food for three days!" We divided the bill between the four of us, and it was not so bad.

My apartment is on the sixth floor of an old building. There is no elevator, so we had to carry all the heavy bags up the stairs. By the time we reached my door, we were all tired and sweaty. Anna laughed and said, "Well, now we really need a good dinner!"

My kitchen is very small. There is only one small table, a sink, a fridge, a stove with two burners, and a rice cooker. Four people in that kitchen is a little crowded. So we decided to work in two teams. Linh and Anna would prepare the spring rolls on the dining table in the living room. Tuan and I would cook the hot dishes in the kitchen.

First, we washed all the vegetables. In Vietnam, we always wash vegetables very carefully, often two or three times. Anna was surprised. "In Australia, we usually just wash them once," she said. Linh explained that the vegetables at the market sometimes have a lot of dirt on them, so it is important to clean them well. After we washed them, we put the herbs and lettuce in a big basket to dry.

Then Tuan started to cook the rice. He put three cups of rice into the pot of the rice cooker, washed it with water two times, and then added fresh water. "How do you know how much water to use?" Anna asked. Tuan smiled and showed her an old trick. He put his finger into the pot, touching the top of the rice. "The water should come up to the first line of your finger," he said. "My mother taught me this. It works every time." Anna tried it too, and she laughed. "That's amazing. No need to measure anything!"

While the rice was cooking, Linh showed Anna how to make spring rolls. First, she boiled the shrimp for three minutes and cut each one in half. She also boiled some pork and cut it into thin slices. Then she cooked the rice noodles in hot water. When everything was ready, she put it all on big plates on the table, next to the herbs, lettuce, and cucumber.

"Now comes the difficult part," Linh said. She took a piece of dry rice paper and put it into a bowl of warm water for just one second. Then she put it on a plate. The rice paper became soft very quickly. She put some lettuce, noodles, herbs, a few slices of pork, and two halves of a shrimp on the paper. Then she folded the sides and rolled it tightly. In less than a minute, she had a perfect spring roll.

Anna tried to make one. Her first roll was a disaster. She left the rice paper in the water for too long, so it became too soft and stuck to the plate. When she tried to roll it, the paper broke, and the noodles fell out. Everyone laughed, including Anna. "This looks easy when you do it!" she said to Linh. "Don't worry," Linh answered. "My first spring rolls were terrible too. Try again."

Anna tried again, and again, and again. By her fifth roll, she was much better. By her tenth roll, it looked almost perfect. She was very proud of herself and took a photo of it with her phone. "I'm going to send this to my mother," she said. "She will not believe that I made it."

In the kitchen, Tuan and I were working on the fish. We bought a big fish, which the seller had already cleaned for us. I cut it into four large pieces and added a little salt. Then I heated some oil in a pan. When the oil was very hot, I carefully put the fish into the pan. The oil made a loud noise, and some of it jumped out of the pan. "Be careful!" Tuan said. I stepped back quickly. Hot oil can give you a bad burn.

I fried the fish for about five minutes on each side, until it was golden and crispy. Meanwhile, Tuan made the tomato sauce. He cut the tomatoes into small pieces and cooked them in another pan with some oil, garlic, and onions. Then he added a little fish sauce, sugar, and water. He let the sauce cook slowly until it was thick and red. When the fish was ready, we put it on a big plate and poured the hot tomato sauce over it. It smelled delicious.

Next was the morning glory. This is a very common vegetable in Vietnam, and almost every family eats it. It is cheap, healthy, and very easy to cook. Tuan heated some oil in the pan and added a lot of chopped garlic. When the garlic turned light brown, he added the morning glory and cooked it on high heat for only two minutes, stirring all the time. Then he added a little salt and fish sauce. "The secret is to cook it fast," he said. "If you cook it too long, it becomes soft and dark."

The last hot dish was the chicken soup. I had already put the chicken in a big pot of water earlier, with some onions, ginger, and salt. It cooked slowly for about forty minutes while we made the other dishes. Now the soup was clear, and it smelled wonderful. I took the chicken out, cut it into pieces, and put it back into the soup with some green onions and pepper.

Meanwhile, Anna and Linh made the mango drink. They peeled three ripe mangoes, cut them into pieces, and put them in a blender with ice, milk, and a little sugar. Anna tasted it and closed her eyes. "This is heaven," she said. We all laughed.

At about eight o'clock, dinner was finally ready. We put all the dishes on the table in the living room: the spring rolls with their dipping sauce, the fried fish with tomato sauce, the morning glory, the chicken soup, and a big bowl of hot rice. It looked like a real feast. We were very hungry, and the food smelled amazing.

Before we started, Anna took a picture of the table. "I want to remember this," she said. Then we sat on the floor around the table, Vietnamese style, and started to eat. Everyone took food from the dishes in the middle and put it in their own bowl. Anna was already very good at using chopsticks. She tried a little bit of everything.

"Which dish do you like best?" Tuan asked her.

Anna thought for a moment. "It is very hard to choose," she said. "The fish is delicious, and the soup is so comforting. But I think the spring rolls are my favorite. Maybe because I made them myself!" Everyone laughed again.

We ate, talked, and laughed for a long time. We talked about our childhood, our families, and the food we ate when we were young. Anna told us about barbecues on the beach in Australia. Tuan told us about his grandmother's cooking in the countryside. Linh told a funny story about the first time she tried to cook rice and burned the whole pot.

After dinner, nobody wanted to wash the dishes. So we made a game of it. We played rock, paper, scissors, and the two losers had to wash. Tuan and I lost. While we washed the dishes in the small kitchen, we could hear Linh and Anna laughing in the living room.

Later that night, after Tuan and Anna went home, I thought about our dinner. It was not a special day. It was not a holiday or a birthday. But it was one of the best evenings I have had in a long time. Good food is wonderful, but good food with good friends is even better. We have already decided to cook together again next month. Next time, Anna will teach us how to make an Australian meat pie.
