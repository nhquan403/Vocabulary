The Rainy Season

In my city, there are only two seasons: the dry season and the rainy season. The dry season lasts from about December to April. During these months, the sky is blue, the air is dry, and it hardly ever rains. The rainy season starts in May and continues until November. During the rainy season, it rains almost every day, usually in the afternoon. Some people hate the rainy season. I used to hate it too. But over the years, I have learned to love it.

The first rain of the season is always special. After months of dry, hot weather, everyone is waiting for it. The streets are dusty, the plants look tired, and the heat makes everyone feel lazy and unhappy. Then one afternoon, the sky suddenly becomes dark. A strong wind starts to blow. Leaves and plastic bags fly through the air. People on the street look up at the sky and start to hurry. Shop owners quickly bring their things inside. Then, with a loud crack of thunder, the rain begins.

The first rain is usually very heavy. It does not start slowly, with a few drops. It comes all at once, like someone has opened a giant tap in the sky. In just a few seconds, the streets are wet. In a few minutes, there is water everywhere. The rain is so loud on the metal roofs that you cannot hear people talking. Children run out into the street to play in the rain, laughing and shouting. Their mothers call them back inside, but they don't listen. Old people sit by their windows and watch. And everyone breathes in the smell of the rain on the dry ground. It is a wonderful smell, fresh and clean. I think it is one of the best smells in the world.

After the first rain, the city changes. The trees become green again. The air becomes cooler and cleaner. Flowers start to bloom. Frogs come out at night and sing loudly in the gardens and the parks. The heat of the dry season is gone, at least for a while, and everybody feels more alive.

But the rainy season also brings problems. The biggest problem in my city is flooding. Our city is very low and close to the sea, and many streets do not have good drains. When it rains heavily, the water has nowhere to go. In some streets, the water can come up to your knees in less than an hour. Motorbikes stop in the middle of the road because water gets into their engines. People have to push their motorbikes through the dirty water. Cars move very slowly and make big waves that splash the people on the sidewalk. Sometimes, the water even comes into houses and shops on the ground floor.

My family lives on a street that floods easily. Every year, during the rainy season, we have to be prepared. My father built a small wall, about thirty centimeters high, at our front door to stop the water from coming in. We keep all our important things on high shelves, not on the floor. When heavy rain is coming, my mother moves the rice, the shoes, and anything electric to a higher place. We also keep candles, a flashlight, and some batteries in a drawer, because sometimes the electricity goes off during big storms.

I remember one terrible night when I was about twelve years old. There was a very big storm, the biggest I had ever seen. It rained for six hours without stopping. The wind was so strong that it broke some branches from the big tree in front of our house. The electricity went off at about eight o'clock, and we sat in the dark with only a few candles. Then the water started to rise in the street. First, it covered the sidewalk. Then it reached the top of my father's small wall. Then it started to come into our house. My parents moved as fast as they could. We carried the television, the computer, the rice, and the clothes upstairs. My grandmother sat on the stairs and prayed. My little brother was only two years old at the time, and he cried all night. By midnight, there was water up to my knees in our living room.

The next morning, the rain stopped, and the water slowly went down. But our house was a mess. There was mud on the floor, on the walls, and on the furniture. Some of our books and photos were ruined. The refrigerator was broken. It took us a whole week to clean everything. Our neighbors had the same problem, so everyone helped each other. People shared food, lent tools, and helped carry heavy furniture. One neighbor who had a big house on higher ground invited three families to stay with him until their homes were dry. I learned a lot about kindness and community that week.

Not every day in the rainy season is like that, of course. Most of the time, the rain comes in the afternoon, falls hard for an hour or two, and then stops. After the rain, the sky is often clear and beautiful. Sometimes there is even a rainbow. Life in the city learns to follow the rain. People plan their day around it. They go out and do their shopping in the morning, when it is usually dry. In the afternoon, when the rain starts, they stay inside, or they wait in a coffee shop until it stops.

Waiting for the rain to stop in a coffee shop is one of my favorite things to do. When you are caught in the rain on the street, you look for a place to hide. Often it is a small coffee shop with a roof over the sidewalk. You sit on a little plastic chair, order a hot coffee or a cup of tea, and watch the rain. There are always other people there too, also waiting. Sometimes you start talking to them. Once, I met an old man who told me about his life as a soldier during the war. Another time, I met a young woman from Japan who was traveling around the country alone. We talked for two hours, and we are still friends today. The rain brings strangers together.

Everyone in my city has a raincoat. It is the most important thing to have during the rainy season. Most raincoats here are made of thin, cheap plastic, and they come in all colors: blue, green, yellow, pink, and red. Many people who ride motorbikes wear big raincoats that cover them and their bikes completely. When it starts to rain, the motorbikes stop at the side of the road, and everyone quickly puts on their raincoats. Then they continue on their way. From above, the streets look like a moving river of colors.

I always keep a raincoat and a small umbrella in my bag, even when the sky is blue in the morning. I learned this lesson the hard way. When I first started university, I did not believe that it could rain so suddenly. One afternoon, I left my raincoat at home because the morning was sunny. At four o'clock, there was a huge storm. I had to walk home in the rain, and I arrived completely wet, with water in my shoes and my books ruined. My mother laughed when she saw me. "Now you know," she said. Since that day, I have never forgotten my raincoat.

The rainy season is also an important time for farmers. In the countryside, the rain fills the rice fields and the rivers. Without the rain, the rice cannot grow. My grandmother grew up on a farm, and she told me that when she was young, farmers prayed for the rain to come on time. If the rain was late, the rice would die, and families would not have enough food. If there was too much rain, the fields would flood, and the rice would also die. So farmers always watched the sky very carefully. For them, the rain was not just weather. It was life.

Now, when the rain starts in the afternoon, I don't feel annoyed anymore. I like to sit by my window with a cup of hot tea and a good book. I listen to the sound of the rain on the roof, watch the water run down the glass, and feel the cool air come into the room. Sometimes I fall asleep in my chair. My grandmother often sits with me. She tells me stories about the rain in her village when she was a girl: how the children caught fish in the flooded rice fields, how the frogs sang all night long, and how her mother made sweet potato soup on rainy evenings.

The rainy season is not always easy. It brings floods, mud, broken trees, and wet shoes. But it also brings green trees, cool air, rainbows, singing frogs, and moments of peace. It reminds us that we cannot control everything. Sometimes, the best thing to do is to sit down, have a cup of tea, and wait for the rain to stop.
