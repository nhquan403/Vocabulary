Staying Healthy

Two years ago, I was not a healthy person. I worked in an office from eight in the morning until six in the evening, sitting at a computer all day. I did not exercise at all. I ate a lot of fast food, fried food, and sweets. I drank three or four cups of coffee every day and almost no water. At night, I stayed up late watching videos on my phone, and I usually slept only five or six hours. I was always tired. I had headaches, back pain, and stomach problems. I gained ten kilos in three years. I knew something was wrong, but I did not have the energy or the motivation to change.

Then, at my company's yearly health checkup, the doctor gave me some bad news. My blood pressure was too high, and so was my blood sugar. "You are only thirty years old," she said, looking at my results with a serious face. "If you continue like this, you will have serious health problems in ten years. You need to change your lifestyle now." Her words scared me. My father had a heart attack when he was fifty-five, and I did not want the same thing to happen to me. That night, I sat at my kitchen table and made a plan to change my life.

I knew that I could not change everything at once. If I tried to do too much too fast, I would give up after a week. So I decided to make small changes, one at a time, and to give myself time to get used to each one. I focused on four areas: exercise, food, sleep, and stress.

I started with exercise, because I thought it would be the hardest. I had not exercised since I was in high school, and I was very unfit. I could not run for more than one minute without feeling like I was going to die. So I started with walking. Every evening after work, I walked for twenty minutes around my neighborhood. At first, it was boring, and I often wanted to stay at home on the sofa. But I listened to music or podcasts while I walked, and slowly, I started to enjoy it. After a month, I increased my walk to thirty minutes. After two months, I started to jog a little during my walks: one minute of jogging, then two minutes of walking, then one minute of jogging again.

After six months, I could run for thirty minutes without stopping. I was very proud of myself. I joined a running club in my neighborhood. We meet three times a week, early in the morning, in a park near the river. There are people of all ages and all levels in the club. Some are very fast, and some are slow like me. But everyone is friendly and encouraging. Running with other people is much more fun than running alone, and it helps me stay motivated. Even when I do not feel like getting up early, I go, because I know my friends are waiting for me.

I also started doing some simple exercises at home: push-ups, sit-ups, and stretching. I found free videos on the internet that show you how to do them correctly. Every morning, I spend ten or fifteen minutes doing these exercises before I take a shower. At work, I also try to move more. I take the stairs instead of the elevator. Every hour, I stand up from my desk, walk around the office, and stretch my back and neck. Some of my colleagues laughed at me at first, but now a few of them do it too.

The second area was food. This was difficult for me, because I love food, especially unhealthy food. I did not want to stop eating everything I enjoyed. So instead of making strict rules, I tried to make better choices most of the time. I started to cook at home more often, so I could control what I was eating. I ate more vegetables and fruit. I changed from white rice to brown rice, which my grandmother says is much better for your health. I ate more fish and chicken and less red meat. I stopped drinking sweet drinks and soda, and I started drinking more water. I bought a big water bottle and kept it on my desk at work, so I would remember to drink. My goal was two liters of water a day.

I did not stop eating sweets and fried food completely. But I ate them less often, and in smaller portions. I learned to enjoy them as a treat, not as an everyday thing. On weekends, I still eat out with my friends and enjoy whatever I want. I believe that a healthy diet should not make you unhappy. It is about balance.

I also learned to eat more slowly. Before, I often ate my lunch in ten minutes in front of my computer while I was working. I did not even notice what I was eating. Now, I take a real lunch break. I sit with my colleagues, put my phone away, and eat slowly. I chew my food well and enjoy every bite. When I eat slowly, I feel full faster, so I eat less. And my stomach problems have almost disappeared.

The third area was sleep. I did not realize how important sleep was until I started to sleep more. My doctor told me that adults need seven to nine hours of sleep every night. When you do not get enough sleep, your body and your brain cannot work well. You feel tired, you cannot concentrate, you get sick more easily, and you are more likely to gain weight. I was sleeping only five or six hours, so I was always tired, and I drank a lot of coffee to stay awake. It was a bad circle.

To sleep better, I made some new rules for myself. I go to bed at the same time every night, at about half past ten, and I get up at the same time every morning, even on weekends. One hour before bed, I turn off my phone and my computer. The light from screens makes it harder to fall asleep. Instead, I read a book, take a warm shower, or listen to quiet music. I also stopped drinking coffee after two o'clock in the afternoon. My bedroom is now only for sleeping. I keep it dark, quiet, and cool. At first, it was hard to fall asleep without my phone. But after a few weeks, I started to fall asleep more quickly and sleep more deeply. Now I wake up in the morning feeling fresh and full of energy, often before my alarm rings.

The fourth area was stress. My job is busy, and there is often a lot of pressure. Before, I took my stress home with me. I worried about work in the evening and on weekends. I could not relax. This was bad for my mind and my body. So I started to look for ways to deal with stress.

Exercise helped a lot. When I run, I forget about my problems. After a run, I always feel calm and happy. I also started to practice deep breathing. When I feel stressed at work, I close my eyes, breathe in slowly for four seconds, hold my breath for four seconds, and breathe out slowly for four seconds. I do this five or six times. It sounds simple, but it really helps me calm down.

I also learned to set limits. I stopped checking my work emails in the evening and on weekends. I learned to say no to extra work when I already had too much. I make time for things I enjoy, like reading, cooking, and spending time with my friends and family. Once a month, I visit my parents in the countryside, where the air is clean and life is slow. Spending time in nature always makes me feel peaceful.

It has been two years since I started making these changes. My life is very different now. I have lost twelve kilos. My blood pressure and blood sugar are normal. I do not have headaches or back pain anymore. I have more energy, and I feel happier and more confident. I get sick much less often. At my last health checkup, the doctor looked at my results and smiled. "Whatever you are doing," she said, "keep doing it."

Of course, I am not perfect. Some weeks, I am too busy to exercise. Some nights, I stay up too late. Sometimes I eat a whole pizza by myself. But that is okay. Being healthy is not about being perfect. It is about making good choices most of the time. When I have a bad day or a bad week, I do not give up. I just start again the next day.

If you want to be healthier, my advice is to start small. Do not try to change everything at once. Choose one small thing, like walking for fifteen minutes a day or drinking more water, and do it every day until it becomes a habit. Then add another small thing. Be patient with yourself. Changes take time. But your body is the only home you will have for your whole life. It is worth taking care of.
