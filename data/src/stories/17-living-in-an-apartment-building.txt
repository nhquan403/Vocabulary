Living in an Apartment Building

Two years ago, I moved out of my parents' house and into my own apartment. It was a big step for me. I was twenty-six years old, I had a stable job, and I wanted to be more independent. My parents were not very happy at first. In my culture, many young people live with their parents until they get married, and sometimes even after that. "Why do you want to pay rent when you can live here for free?" my mother asked. But I explained that I wanted to learn to take care of myself. In the end, my parents understood, and they even helped me move.

Finding an apartment was not easy. I spent almost two months looking. I searched on websites, asked my friends, and visited more than fifteen apartments. Some were too expensive. Some were too far from my office. Some were dark, dirty, or noisy. One had a nice view, but the bathroom had no window and smelled terrible. Another was perfect, but the landlord did not allow pets, and I wanted to have a cat one day. I was starting to feel tired and hopeless. Then my colleague told me about an empty apartment in her building. I went to see it the next day, and I knew immediately that it was the right place for me.

My apartment is on the eighth floor of a fifteen-floor building. It is not very big. There is a living room with a small kitchen, one bedroom, a bathroom, and a small balcony. But it is bright and clean, and it has big windows. From my balcony, I can see the city and, in the distance, the river. At night, the lights of the city are beautiful. The building is about fifteen minutes from my office by bus, and there is a supermarket, a market, a park, and many small restaurants nearby. The rent is a little high, but I can afford it if I am careful with my money.

When I moved in, the apartment was empty. I had to buy everything: a bed, a sofa, a table, chairs, a fridge, a washing machine, pots, pans, plates, and many other things. I did not have much money, so I bought some things second-hand. I found a nice wooden table and four chairs online from a family who was moving to another country. My aunt gave me her old sofa. My parents bought me a new bed as a gift. Slowly, the empty apartment became a home. I put plants on the balcony, photos on the walls, and books on the shelves. I painted one wall in the living room a light green color. It took me a whole weekend, and I got paint all over my clothes and my hair, but I was very proud of the result.

Living alone was harder than I expected. At my parents' house, my mother cooked, cleaned, and did the laundry. I never had to think about these things. Now, I had to do everything myself. In the first few weeks, I ate instant noodles almost every night because I was too tired to cook after work. My laundry piled up in the corner of my bedroom. I forgot to buy toilet paper, and I forgot to pay my electricity bill. Once, I locked myself out of my apartment, and I had to wait three hours for someone to come and open the door. I called my mother almost every day to ask for advice. "How do I clean the bathroom? How long do I cook rice? What do I do if the sink is blocked?" My mother laughed, but she always helped me. "Now you understand how much work I did for you all those years," she said.

Slowly, I learned. I made a weekly plan for cooking, cleaning, and shopping. On Sunday, I go to the market and buy food for the whole week. I cook a few dishes and put them in the fridge, so I only have to heat them up after work. I clean the apartment on Saturday morning and do the laundry on Wednesday evening. I set reminders on my phone to pay my bills. It sounds boring, but it makes my life much easier.

One of the best things about living in an apartment building is my neighbors. There are eight apartments on my floor, and I know almost everyone now. Across the hall, there is an old couple, Mr. and Mrs. Lam. They are retired teachers, and they have lived in the building for more than fifteen years. They are very kind to me. Mrs. Lam often brings me food, because she thinks I am too thin. Mr. Lam helped me fix my door when it would not close properly. In return, I help them with their phones and their computer, because they are not very good with technology. Last month, I taught Mrs. Lam how to make video calls, so now she can see her grandchildren who live in another country. She was so happy that she cried.

Next door to me, there is a young family with two small children. The children are very cute, but they are also very loud. Sometimes, early on Sunday mornings, I can hear them running, jumping, and shouting through the wall. At first, this annoyed me. But one day, the mother knocked on my door with a plate of cake. She said sorry for the noise and asked me if it was a problem. I could not be angry with her. Now, when I hear the children in the morning, I just smile and put on my headphones. Sometimes, the children come to my door and ask to see my plants on the balcony. They love to help me water them.

There is also a young man who lives at the end of the hall. He is a musician, and he plays the piano. At first, I was worried that it would be noisy, but he plays very beautifully, and only in the early evening. Now, I look forward to hearing him play when I come home from work. Sometimes, I open my door so I can hear the music better while I cook dinner.

Of course, living in an apartment building is not always perfect. There are some problems. The elevator is old, and it breaks down about once a month. When that happens, I have to climb eight floors of stairs, carrying my shopping bags. It is good exercise, but it is not much fun, especially in the hot season. The walls are thin, so you can hear your neighbors talking, watching television, or arguing. Some people do not follow the building rules. They leave their trash in the hallway, park their motorbikes in the wrong places, or play loud music late at night. Once, there was a water leak from the apartment above mine, and water came through my bathroom ceiling. It took two weeks to fix.

Our building has a manager, a friendly middle-aged man who sits at a desk on the ground floor. He takes care of the building, collects the monthly fees, and helps when there is a problem. He also keeps our packages when we are not at home. Every morning, he says good morning to everyone, and he knows everybody's name. He also knows everybody's business. If you want to know anything about anyone in the building, you just have to ask him.

There are also some nice shared spaces in the building. On the ground floor, there is a small garden with benches and a children's playground. In the evening, many families come down to the garden. The children play, the parents talk, and the old people sit on the benches and watch. On the roof, there is a small area where people dry their clothes and grow vegetables in pots. Some old residents meet there every morning at six o'clock to do exercise together. Mrs. Lam invited me to join them once. I went, but I could not keep up with them. They were much more flexible and energetic than me!

Once a year, before Tet, the building has a big party in the garden. Everyone brings food, and the building manager organizes games for the children and music for the adults. It is a great chance to meet the people you see in the elevator every day but never talk to. Last year, I met a woman from the twelfth floor who works in the same company as me, but in a different department. Now we take the bus to work together every morning.

Before I moved here, I thought that living in the city would be lonely. I thought that people in big buildings did not know or care about their neighbors. But I was wrong. My building is like a small village. People help each other, share food, watch each other's children, and look out for each other. When I was sick last winter, Mrs. Lam brought me soup every day, and the children next door made me a get-well card with drawings of flowers and cats.

I still visit my parents every weekend, and I still call my mother for advice sometimes. But I have learned to take care of myself, and I have built a new life here. My small apartment on the eighth floor is not just a place to sleep. It is my home.
