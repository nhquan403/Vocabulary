A Day with My Family

My name is Mai, and I live with my family in a small house near the center of the city. There are five people in my family: my father, my mother, my younger brother Nam, my grandmother, and me. We also have a fat orange cat called Bun. Our house is not big, but it is warm and full of life. I want to tell you about a normal day in our home, because I think normal days are the most important days of all.

Every morning, my grandmother is the first person to wake up. She gets up at five o'clock, before the sun rises. She says that old people do not need much sleep. She goes to the kitchen, boils some water, and makes a pot of green tea. Then she sits by the window and watches the street. She likes to see the people who walk past our house: the woman who sells bread, the man who rides his bike to the market, and the children in their school uniforms. When I ask her why she gets up so early, she smiles and says, "The morning is the best part of the day. The air is fresh, and the world is quiet."

My mother wakes up at half past five. She is a nurse at the hospital near our house, so she has to start work early. Before she leaves, she cooks breakfast for all of us. On most days, we eat rice soup or noodles. Sometimes she makes eggs and bread, because my brother loves them. My mother is a very busy woman, but she never forgets to cook for her family. She always says, "A good day starts with a good breakfast."

My father gets up at six. He works in an office in the city. He is a quiet man who does not talk much in the morning. He drinks a cup of black coffee, reads the news on his phone, and asks us about our plans for the day. Then he takes my brother to school on his motorbike. My brother is ten years old, and he is in the fourth grade. He is a funny and lively boy, but he is not good at waking up. Every morning, my mother has to call his name three or four times. "Nam! Get up! You will be late!" And every morning, he says, "Five more minutes, Mom!"

I am twenty years old, and I am a student at a university. My classes usually start at eight o'clock, so I leave the house at half past seven. I go to school by bus. The bus is often crowded, but I do not mind. I use the time to read a book or listen to music. Sometimes I look out of the window and think about my day.

At university, I study business. I like my classes, and I have many good friends there. My best friend is Lan. We have been friends since we were in high school. She is kind, clever, and very funny. We often have lunch together in the school cafeteria. The food there is cheap and quite good. After lunch, we sit under a big tree in the garden and talk about our classes, our families, and our dreams for the future. Lan wants to be a teacher. I want to open my own small coffee shop one day.

While I am at school, my grandmother stays at home. She is seventy-five years old, but she is still very active. She cleans the house, waters the plants, and feeds the cat. She also goes to the market every morning to buy fresh vegetables, meat, and fish. Everyone at the market knows her. They call her "Grandma Hoa." She likes to talk with the sellers and ask about their families. When she comes home, she often brings a small gift for us, like a bag of sweet fruit or some cakes.

In the afternoon, my grandmother takes a short nap. Then she listens to the radio or watches old movies on television. Sometimes her friends from the neighborhood come to visit. They sit in our living room, drink tea, eat sunflower seeds, and talk for hours. I love to hear them laugh. They talk about the past, about their children and grandchildren, and about the changes in our city. "When I was young," my grandmother often says, "there were no tall buildings here. There were only small houses, rice fields, and trees."

My brother comes home from school at four o'clock. He is always hungry when he gets home. He throws his school bag on the floor and runs to the kitchen to look for something to eat. My grandmother usually gives him a glass of milk and a piece of cake. Then she tells him to do his homework. My brother does not like homework. He would rather play games on his computer or play football with his friends in the street. But my grandmother is strict. "Homework first, then games," she says. And my brother always listens to her, because he loves her very much.

I come home at about five o'clock. If I have time, I help my brother with his math homework. He is good at English, but math is difficult for him. I try to explain things slowly and clearly. Sometimes he understands quickly, and sometimes he gets angry and says, "This is too hard!" Then I tell him, "Don't worry. Let's try again together." In the end, he always finishes his homework, and he is proud of himself.

My mother gets home at six. She is tired after a long day at the hospital, but she still smiles when she sees us. She takes a shower, changes her clothes, and then starts to cook dinner. My grandmother and I help her in the kitchen. My grandmother washes the vegetables, and I cut the meat. My mother is in charge of the cooking. She is an excellent cook. Her fish soup is the best in the world, I think.

My father comes home at about half past six. He usually brings some fruit or a newspaper. He sits in the living room and talks with my brother about his day at school. Sometimes they play a game of chess. My father is very good at chess, but sometimes he lets my brother win. My brother does not know this, and he is always very happy when he wins.

At seven o'clock, we have dinner together. This is my favorite time of the day. We sit around a round table in the kitchen. There is always rice, a soup, some vegetables, and a meat or fish dish. Before we eat, we wait for my grandmother to pick up her chopsticks first. This is a family custom, and it shows our respect for her. During dinner, we talk about everything. My father talks about his work. My mother tells us about her patients. My brother talks about his friends and his teacher. I tell them about my classes and about Lan. My grandmother listens to everyone and gives us advice. We laugh a lot at the dinner table.

After dinner, my brother and I wash the dishes. We do not always enjoy this job, but it is our duty. My brother washes, and I dry. Sometimes we play music on my phone and sing while we work. My parents sit in the living room and watch the news on television. My grandmother cuts some fruit for dessert. Then we all sit together, eat fruit, and talk a little more.

At nine o'clock, my brother goes to bed. He brushes his teeth, puts on his pajamas, and asks my grandmother to tell him a story. My grandmother knows many old stories about kings, dragons, and clever animals. My brother has heard most of them many times, but he still loves to hear them again. He usually falls asleep before the story ends.

I study in my room until about eleven o'clock. My room is small, but it is my favorite place in the house. There is a desk by the window, a bookshelf full of books, and a soft bed with a blue blanket. On the wall, there are photos of my family and friends. When I feel tired, I stop studying and look at the photos. They make me smile.

Before I go to sleep, I often think about my day. My family is not rich, and our life is simple. We do not have a big car or a beautiful garden. But we have each other. We eat together, laugh together, and help each other every day. When I am sad, my mother hugs me. When I have a problem, my father listens to me. When I am tired, my grandmother makes me a cup of hot tea. When I feel lonely, my little brother makes me laugh.

Some of my friends want to move to another country in the future. They dream about big cities, good jobs, and a lot of money. I understand them, and I wish them luck. But I know that I want to stay close to my family. For me, happiness is not about money. Happiness is a warm meal with the people you love, a cup of tea in the morning, and a quiet house at night. Happiness is a normal day with my family.
