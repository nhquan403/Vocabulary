My First Day at a New Job

I will never forget my first day at my new job. It was a Monday in early September. The weather was still hot, but the mornings were starting to feel a little cooler. I was twenty-three years old, and I had just finished university. For three months, I had looked for a job. I sent my application to more than thirty companies. Some never answered. Some invited me for an interview but then chose someone else. I was starting to feel hopeless. Then, one afternoon, my phone rang. It was a woman from a small travel company. "We would like to offer you the job," she said. I was so happy that I almost dropped my phone.

The night before my first day, I could not sleep. I was excited, but I was also very nervous. I worried about everything. What should I wear? What if I was late? What if my boss did not like me? What if I made a terrible mistake? I lay in bed and looked at the ceiling for hours. At about two o'clock in the morning, I finally fell asleep.

My alarm rang at six o'clock. I got up immediately, took a quick shower, and put on the clothes I had prepared the night before: a white shirt, dark blue pants, and black shoes. My mother had ironed my shirt for me. She also made me a big breakfast, but I was too nervous to eat much. I only ate a little bread and drank a glass of milk. My mother hugged me at the door. "Don't worry," she said. "Just be yourself, and be polite. Everything will be fine."

The office was in a tall building in the center of the city. I took the bus, and I arrived thirty minutes early. I did not want to go in too early, so I walked around the block two times. Then I bought a coffee from a small shop on the corner and drank it slowly. At ten minutes to eight, I took a deep breath and walked into the building.

The company was on the twelfth floor. When the elevator doors opened, I saw a small, bright office with big windows. There were about fifteen desks, some plants, and a long table in the middle of the room. On the walls, there were beautiful photos of mountains, beaches, and old towns. A young woman at the front desk smiled at me. "Good morning! You must be the new person," she said. "Welcome! My name is Thu. I'm the receptionist."

Thu took me to meet my manager, Mr. Duc. He was a man of about forty-five, with short gray hair and glasses. He had a serious face, and at first, I was a little afraid of him. But when he started to talk, he was very friendly and kind. "Welcome to our team," he said, shaking my hand. "We are a small company, so everyone here is like family. Don't be afraid to ask questions. Nobody expects you to know everything on your first day."

Mr. Duc showed me my desk. It was next to a window, and I could see the river and the bridge from my chair. On the desk, there was a computer, a phone, a notebook, some pens, and a small plant. There was also a card that said, "Welcome to the team!" Everyone in the office had signed it. I felt very touched.

Then Mr. Duc introduced me to my colleagues. There was Hai, who planned tours to the mountains in the north. There was Phuong, who worked with hotels and restaurants. There was Kevin, an American man who wrote all the English information on the company website. And there was Ha, a woman about my age who had joined the company six months before me. Mr. Duc told me that Ha would help me during my first few weeks.

Ha was very friendly. She explained how the company worked and what my job would be. My main job was to answer emails and phone calls from customers, help them choose a tour, and book their hotels and tickets. Many of our customers were foreign tourists, so I needed to use English every day. This made me a little nervous, because my English was good, but not perfect.

"Don't worry," Ha said. "I was nervous too when I started. Just speak slowly and clearly. If you don't understand something, ask the customer to repeat it. Most people are very patient."

For the first two hours, Ha showed me how to use the computer system. It was quite complicated. There were many different pages and buttons, and I had to remember a lot of passwords. I wrote everything down in my notebook. Sometimes I felt lost, but Ha was very patient. She explained things two or three times when I did not understand.

At ten o'clock, the phone on my desk rang. Ha looked at me and smiled. "Do you want to try?" she asked. My heart started to beat very fast. I picked up the phone. "Good morning, this is Green Path Travel. How can I help you?" I said. My voice sounded strange, but I kept going.

The caller was a woman from England. She wanted to book a two-day tour to a famous bay for her family. She spoke very fast, and she had an accent that I was not used to. I asked her to speak more slowly, and she did. I asked her about the dates, the number of people, and the kind of hotel she wanted. I wrote everything down carefully. Ha sat next to me and helped me find the right information on the computer. After about fifteen minutes, everything was ready. The woman thanked me and said, "You've been very helpful." When I put down the phone, my hands were shaking, but I felt very proud.

"See? You did a great job," Ha said.

At twelve o'clock, it was time for lunch. Everyone in the office usually eats together. Some people bring food from home, and some people buy food from the small restaurants near the building. That day, my colleagues took me to a noodle shop on the next street. It was a small, noisy place with plastic chairs and tables, but the food was excellent. We all ordered beef noodle soup. While we ate, my colleagues asked me about my family, my studies, and my hobbies. They also told me funny stories about the company and about some of their strangest customers.

Kevin told us about a customer who once asked him if there were any tigers in the city. Hai told us about a tour group who got lost in the mountains because they did not want to follow the guide. Everyone laughed. I started to feel more relaxed. My colleagues were not scary at all. They were normal, friendly people, just like me.

After lunch, I worked on emails. There were many questions from customers. Some wanted to know about prices. Some wanted to change the dates of their tours. One man wanted to know if he could bring his dog on a boat trip. I did not know the answer to many of the questions, so I asked Ha or Mr. Duc. They always helped me. Slowly, I started to understand how everything worked.

At around three o'clock, I made my first mistake. I sent an email to the wrong customer. It was a list of hotel prices for a family of five, but I sent it to a young couple who had asked about a tour for two people. When I realized what I had done, my face turned red. I felt terrible. I went to Mr. Duc and told him what happened. I was sure that he would be angry.

But Mr. Duc only smiled. "Everyone makes mistakes," he said. "The important thing is that you told me. Just send the couple a new email and say sorry. Then send the right email to the family." I did what he said, and the problem was solved in five minutes. The young couple even wrote back and said, "No problem! Have a nice day!" I learned an important lesson that day. It is okay to make mistakes, but you must be honest about them.

At five thirty, it was time to go home. I was very tired. My head was full of new information, new names, and new passwords. But I was also very happy. Before I left, Mr. Duc came to my desk. "How was your first day?" he asked. "It was difficult, but I enjoyed it," I answered honestly. He nodded. "That's a good answer. Tomorrow will be easier. See you in the morning."

On the bus home, I looked out of the window at the busy streets and thought about my day. In the morning, I was so nervous that I could not eat. Now, I felt like I belonged somewhere. When I got home, my mother was waiting for me with dinner. "So?" she asked. "How was it?"

I smiled and said, "Mom, I think I'm going to like this job."

That was three years ago. Today, I am still working at the same company. I have learned a lot, and I have made many good friends. Ha is now one of my best friends. Last month, a new young man joined our team. On his first day, he looked very nervous. I went to his desk, smiled, and said, "Welcome to the team. Don't be afraid to ask questions. Nobody expects you to know everything on your first day."
