A Garden on the Balcony

When I moved into my small apartment in the city, the first thing I noticed was the balcony. It was not very big, only about two meters wide and three meters long. But it got sunlight all morning, and it had a view of a small park across the street. The previous owner had left it empty, except for some old plastic chairs and a lot of dust. I stood there on my first evening and thought, "This could be a garden."

I grew up in the countryside, where my parents had a big garden with vegetables, fruit trees, and flowers. As a child, I helped my mother plant seeds, pull weeds, and pick vegetables for dinner. I loved the feeling of soil in my hands and the excitement of seeing the first green leaves come out of the ground. When I moved to the city for university, I missed that feeling very much. The city was full of buildings, roads, and motorbikes, but there were not many plants. Now that I had my own balcony, I could finally have a small piece of nature again.

But I did not know where to start. Growing plants on a balcony in the city is very different from growing them in a big garden in the countryside. There is not much space. The soil has to be carried up in bags. The wind can be strong on high floors. And the plants depend completely on you for water. So I called my mother and asked for her advice.

"Start small," she said. "Choose a few easy plants first. Herbs and some simple vegetables are best. When you have more experience, you can add more." She also told me some important things. "Every plant needs four things: sunlight, water, good soil, and love." I laughed at the last one, but she was serious. "Plants know when you care about them," she said.

The next weekend, I went to a garden shop near my apartment. It was a small, crowded shop full of plants, pots, seeds, tools, and bags of soil. The owner was a friendly old man with dirt under his fingernails and a big smile. When I told him about my balcony, he was very interested. He asked me many questions. How many hours of sunlight did my balcony get? Which direction did it face? How big was it? How much time did I have to take care of plants? Then he helped me choose what to buy.

I bought six medium-sized pots, two long boxes for planting, three big bags of soil, a small bag of natural fertilizer, a watering can, a pair of gardening gloves, and a small shovel. For plants, he suggested that I start with some herbs: basil, mint, green onions, and coriander. These are used in many Vietnamese dishes, and they are easy to grow. He also suggested tomatoes, chili peppers, and lettuce. Some of them I bought as young plants, and some I bought as seeds. He also gave me some free seeds for morning glory. "Very easy," he said. "It grows like crazy."

When I got home, I spent the whole afternoon setting up my garden. I cleaned the balcony and threw away the old plastic chairs. I put a few small stones at the bottom of each pot so the water could drain out. Then I filled the pots and boxes with soil and mixed in some fertilizer. I planted the young plants carefully, pressing the soil gently around their roots. Then I planted the seeds in the long boxes, about one centimeter deep, in neat rows. Finally, I watered everything. When I finished, I was dirty and tired, but very happy. My balcony did not look like much yet, just some pots of soil and a few small green plants. But I could already imagine what it would become.

Every morning before work, I went out to the balcony to check on my garden. I watered the plants, looked at their leaves, and talked to them a little. I know it sounds strange, but I remembered my mother's words about love. After about one week, the first seeds started to come up. Tiny green leaves pushed up through the soil. I was so excited that I took a photo and sent it to my mother. She sent back a happy face and the words, "Good job! Now be patient."

The first few months were a learning experience. Not everything went well. The coriander did not like the hot afternoon sun, and it died after a few weeks. The lettuce grew well at first, but then small insects ate holes in all the leaves. One day, there was a big storm, and the strong wind knocked over two of my pots. The soil spilled everywhere, and one of my tomato plants broke. I was very sad. I called my mother, and she said, "Every gardener loses plants. Don't give up. Learn from it." So I moved the plants that needed less sun to a shadier corner. I learned to make a natural spray with garlic and chili to keep the insects away. I tied my tomato plants to sticks to make them stronger, and I moved the pots closer to the wall to protect them from the wind.

Slowly, my garden grew. The basil and mint grew so well that I had more than I could use. The green onions came back every time I cut them. The morning glory, just as the shop owner had said, grew like crazy. And then, one morning, I saw a small yellow flower on one of my tomato plants. A few weeks later, there was a tiny green tomato. I watched it grow bigger every day. Slowly, it changed color from green to orange to red. When it was finally ripe, I picked it and ate it right there on the balcony. It was warm from the sun, sweet, and juicy. It was the best tomato I had ever tasted. Maybe it was not really better than a tomato from the market. But I had grown it myself, and that made it special.

My balcony garden has changed my life in many small ways. First, it has changed the way I eat. Now I cook at home much more often, because I want to use my fresh herbs and vegetables. Every time I make soup, noodles, or spring rolls, I go out to the balcony and pick a few leaves of basil or mint. The food tastes fresher and more delicious. I also spend less money on vegetables and herbs, although I have to admit that I have spent quite a lot of money on new pots and plants.

Second, my garden helps me relax. My job is stressful, and I spend most of my day in front of a computer. When I come home in the evening, I go out to the balcony, water my plants, and pull out any weeds. It is quiet and peaceful. I can feel my stress slowly going away. Some evenings, I just sit on a small chair among my plants with a cup of tea and watch the sun go down over the park. It is my favorite place in the whole city.

Third, my garden has helped me meet people. My neighbor on the next balcony is an old woman who also loves plants. At first, we only said hello to each other across the wall. Then one day, she asked me about my tomatoes, and we started talking. Now, we share seeds, plants, and advice. She taught me how to grow ginger from a piece of ginger root, and I gave her some of my basil. Sometimes, she gives me fruit from her lemon tree. A few other people in my building have also started their own balcony gardens after seeing mine. We even started a small group online, where we share photos and ask each other questions.

Now, two years later, my balcony is full of green. There are about twenty different plants: herbs, vegetables, a small lemon tree, some colorful flowers, and a few hanging plants that grow down from the roof. Butterflies and bees visit every day. Once, a small bird even built a nest in one of my hanging plants. I was so happy that I did not touch that plant for weeks, until the baby birds had grown and flown away.

You do not need a big garden to grow plants. You do not need much money or much experience. You only need a little space, some sunlight, some patience, and, as my mother says, a little love. If you have a balcony, a window, or even a small table near a window, try growing something. Start with one pot of basil or mint. Water it, watch it grow, and talk to it if you like. I promise that it will make you happy.
